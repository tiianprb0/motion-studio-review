# V5 — narrative direction and layout contract

## Audience first
The viewer is torn between empathizing with a partner and accepting behavior that hurts them. Help them recognize the distinction without diagnosing either person. Show emotional change through action; do not label every sentence with another headline.

## Continuous acts, not one scene per sentence
1. Hook (0–5.4): one key contrast. Two people, emotional closeness, space for self-respect. One short title only.
2. Incident (5.4–12): wide bedroom, phone picked up, angled device insert. Simple mitten hands only; never floating fingers.
3. Understanding (12–26.6): concern → the woman responds → reassurance. Shift from the partner’s reaction to a two-shot. No cloud and heart competing on top of one another.
4. Empathy (26.6–34.1): side-angle seated conversation, listening rather than another repeated standing tableau.
5. Repetition and boundary (34.1–44.8): one fast change of time, the same behavior triggers a new reaction. Woman raises a calm stopping palm and takes ownership of her phone. No grid of duplicate scenes.
6. Recognizing the pattern (44.8–53): overhead trail of incidents, one person recognizes the loop. One minimal label after the visual reveal.
7. Accountability (53–69.7): continuous behavioral sequence. Return phone → listen to stated boundary → start to reach, pause, withdraw → ask first. The phone stays untouched. Do not substitute three labels for demonstrated behavior.
8. Closing (69.7–76.4): comfortable space between the pair; calm acceptance of the boundary. No generic growing plant.
9. Outro: clean horizontal wordmark assembly; the dot remains in the wordmark. No unexplained dot flight. Social handles enter as a second beat.

## Layout contract
Frame 1080×1920. Logo reserve: x735..1005, y110..225. Caption reserve: x100..980, y1480..1700, centered x540. Any short hook/type is limited to y270..500 and disappears before the narrative takes over. Principal humans and props must stay within x75..1005, y570..1430 in wide shots. No arbitrary clip-mask to hide layout errors. Props may overlap hands only when grasped, never faces/captions. Background layers may overlap deliberately. Reaction close-ups and device inserts are named cuts, not accidental crops.

## Review
Check moving proof, then sample entrances, mid-action, transitions and final holds. Evaluate geometry against the reserved zones. Inspect emotion, caption meaning-unit breaks and scene variation. Technical rendering is not a creative-quality pass. Preserve prior versions and the existing recording.
